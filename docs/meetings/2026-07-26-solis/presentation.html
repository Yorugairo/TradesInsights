Issued permits
Nine counties. The commodity layer everyone has — still useful for residential timing, generally too late for commercial.
Discipline
Genuine records only
Captured the way a person would, from public sources. No bot-driving, no bypassing access controls, no exceptions — including when it would be convenient.

Every captured report must reconcile against its own printed totals before a single record is emitted. If the arithmetic disagrees we suppress the source rather than ship a mis-parse — a wrong lead costs you more than a missing one.

The clock, per trade

Residential — after issuance

Commercial — before issuance
  • 2 to 6 months ahead of issuance, during guaranteed-price buyout

Every opportunity carries its own window line on the correct track — bid now, opens in N weeks, or likely closed — so the feed tells you when to act, not merely what exists.

A setting worth fixing

Distance is the wrong filter.
The job is the filter.

We currently down-weight King as distant volume. But a drive is not expensive or cheap on its own — it is expensive relative to what you drove past.

Worth the drive
Specialist work, anywhere
Data centres in Quincy or Moses Lake. Rated shaftwall, STC assemblies, Level 5 in Medina or Mercer Island. Commercial TI in Bellevue. Few people bid these, they pay for the mileage, and there is no local equivalent to pass up.
Not worth the drive
Generic hanging, far away
Standard Level 4 residential in King. You would drive past dozens of Pierce and Thurston jobs of the same value to reach it. The problem is not that King is far — it is the opportunity cost of everything you passed.

So the fix is qualification, not geography. Instead of down-weighting a whole county, let distance be paid for by job type: a specialist assembly justifies any drive in the territory, generic work has to be close. That also means territory should not be a hard county list — Quincy is far outside it and might be the best job on the board.

Appendix · market access

The route into Tier-1
commercial bidding.

Finding the work is our half. This is the half that decides whether you are allowed to bid it — and none of it happens quickly.

  • Prequalify with Tier-1 GCs. Turner, Skanska, Lease Crutcher Lewis, Mortenson, GLY, DPR, Howard S. Wright — via Highwire, COMPASS or Vertikal RMS. Financials, EMR, bonding, $2M–$5M+ general liability.
  • Safety & quality documentation. Written site-specific safety plan, orientation records, EMR under 1.0. Mandatory on tech and industrial sites.
  • Manufacturer certification. USG, National Gypsum, CertainTeed, ROCKWOOL, Trim-Tex. Firestop and acoustic credentials weigh in bid evaluation.
  • Surety bonding capacity. $1M single / $3M–$5M aggregate for performance and payment bonds — required for data centre and public commercial work.
  • Public procurement registration. WA Department of Enterprise Services Bonfire portal and the MRSC Small Works Roster, for automated public, educational and municipal RFPs.

Step 5 is the cheap one and it is worth doing this month — registration is free and turns public work into inbound RFPs rather than something to hunt.

Appendix · software concept — nothing built yet

Two systems worth building.

Field communication — fast to build
  • Daily logs: board counts, linear feet taped, crew hours
  • Photo punch lists with pins dropped on drawings
  • Change orders: photo, description, signature — approved in the field
  • Offline-first, so basements and data centres still log

This is the lever from A4 that actually recovers money — the 10–15% currently lost to verbal agreements.

AI takeoff — genuinely hard
  • Blueprints are noisy, multi-layered and non-standard
  • Scale calibration, vector vs scanned raster, wall-type classification
  • Opening deductions and floor-to-deck height variance
  • 80–90% automation with human review is realistic; full black-box is not

Estimator keeps the pen: AI proposes wall polylines, the human adjusts handles and reclassifies partitions.

Concepts only — neither exists today. Shown so you can tell us whether either is worth more to you than more leads.

Appendix · why they belong together

Estimate, then measure
yourself against it.

Step one
AI takeoff estimate
Linear feet × wall height × layers, minus openings. Studs at 16″ o.c., track, insulation, compound, tape — all derived, none re-keyed.
Step two
Billed job scope
The estimate becomes the contract, and the procurement list goes straight to the supplier without anyone retyping it.
Step three
Daily field logs
Crews log actual boards hung against the model, from the same database.

The loop closes here. Estimated versus actual variance surfaces material waste or lagging productivity while the job is still running — not in a post-mortem after the money is gone.

Same architecture as the lead engine: one database, evidence kept with the decision, variance surfaced rather than averaged away.
